# National Immunization Schedule (Universal Immunisation Programme) — CONDENSED TEACHING EXTRACT

> **DEMONSTRATION DATA.** Condensed extract prepared for a CME teaching demo.
> Not a substitute for the current official UIP schedule. Verify against the
> latest MoHFW / state immunisation handbook before any clinical or programme use.

Source: Universal Immunisation Programme, Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, Government of India.

## [S1] Vaccines at birth

BCG, Hepatitis B birth dose, and OPV-0 are given at birth, or as early as possible within 24 hours for the Hepatitis B birth dose. BCG can be given up to one year of age if missed. OPV-0 is given only within the first 15 days.

## [S2] Primary schedule, 6 to 14 weeks

At 6, 10 and 14 weeks the infant receives OPV-1/2/3 and Pentavalent-1/2/3. Rotavirus vaccine is given at 6, 10 and 14 weeks in states where introduced. Fractional IPV is given at 6 and 14 weeks intradermally. Pneumococcal conjugate vaccine is given at 6 and 14 weeks with a booster at 9 months in states where introduced.

## [S3] Nine to twelve months

Measles-Rubella first dose is given at 9 to 12 completed months. Japanese Encephalitis first dose is given at 9 to 12 months in endemic districts only. Vitamin A first dose is given at 9 months.

## [S4] Sixteen to twenty-four months

Measles-Rubella second dose, DPT first booster, OPV booster, and Japanese Encephalitis second dose in endemic districts are all given at 16 to 24 months. Vitamin A doses 2 to 9 are given every 6 months up to 5 years of age.

## [S5] School-age and adolescent doses

DPT second booster is given at 5 to 6 years. Tetanus and adult diphtheria vaccine is given at 10 years and again at 16 years.

## [S6] Maternal immunisation

Td is given early in pregnancy, with a second dose four weeks after the first. A single Td booster dose is sufficient if the woman received two Td doses in a pregnancy within the last three years.

## [S7] Contraindications and precautions

Minor illness, low-grade fever, and mild diarrhoea are not contraindications to vaccination. Severe anaphylaxis to a previous dose of the same vaccine is an absolute contraindication. Live vaccines are deferred in severe immunosuppression.
